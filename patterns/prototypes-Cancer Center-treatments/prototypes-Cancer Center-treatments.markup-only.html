Chemotherapy

  • Parent
  • Child
  • Current: Child

At Mays Cancer Center, home to UT Health San Antonio MD Anderson, you can receive some of the latest advances in chemotherapy drugs.

Our leading cancer experts use a full spectrum of FDA-approved and new, experimental chemotherapy drugs to treat all cancer types. A focus on families' needs means you can access expert care easily, with your support network by your side.

What is chemotherapy?

Chemotherapy is a category of medicines that destroy cancer cells or stop them from growing and spreading.

Chemotherapy is one way doctors treat cancer. Your doctor may also recommend cancer immunotherapy or targeted therapy drugs depending on your diagnosis and treatment plan.

Types of chemotherapy

Your doctor may recommend chemotherapy at different points in your care. Common approaches include:

  • Neoadjuvant chemotherapy: Medicine you receive before surgery to help shrink a tumor.
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy: Medicine you receive after surgery to help rid the body of cancer cells remaining after surgery.
  • Combination chemotherapy: One or more chemotherapy drugs that may be used with cancer surgery, radiation therapy or other cancer treatments.

Why choose us for chemotherapy?

Our chemotherapy program brings together national research leadership, coordinated care and personalized treatment planning so your care can move forward with the right support around you.

  • Commitment to excellence: As the only National Cancer Institute-designated cancer center in Central and South Texas, Mays Cancer Center excels in cancer care and research.
  • Leading therapies: Our internationally recognized research program helps patients access the standard of care plus new treatment options they may be eligible for.
  • Coordinated care: You can receive chemotherapy services at Mays Cancer Center in San Antonio, where your doctors and support professionals work together across your care.
  • Personalized treatment plan: Cancer doctors with different areas of expertise meet often to determine which therapies, and in which order, are most appropriate for your needs.

Chemotherapy: What to expect

Chemotherapy is a systemic cancer treatment. Chemotherapy drugs travel through your bloodstream to treat cancer throughout the body.

Ways you might receive chemotherapy

You might receive chemotherapy in several ways, depending on the medicines used and your treatment plan.

  • Infusion: A nurse places an IV into a vein in your hand or arm so medicine can enter your bloodstream.
  • Port: A radiologist or surgeon places a small device under your skin so your care team can deliver medication without repeated needle sticks.
  • Central line: A surgeon places a long, thin tube under the skin that a nurse can use to deliver medication.
  • Pills: Some chemotherapy medications come in pills or capsules you swallow, called oral chemotherapy.
  • Under the skin: A member of your care team injects medicine right under your skin. This is called subcutaneous delivery.
  • Breathing in: Some chemotherapy drugs can be inhaled to deliver medicine directly to an affected area, such as the lungs.

About our infusion center

You can receive chemotherapy services in the infusion center at Mays Cancer Center. Our infusion center is set up with comfortable chairs, televisions, Wi-Fi and ample space for you to bring a family member or friend along for support.

Our doctors and oncology-trained nurses have decades of experience working with chemotherapy drugs. They use this knowledge to help you avoid or manage side effects, such as nausea.

How to talk to your doctor about chemotherapy

Your care team will consider many factors, such as the type of cancer and your overall health, when deciding which chemotherapy drugs are right for you. You might receive one or more chemotherapy drugs at one time.

Before you start chemotherapy, our team will sit down with you to explain what your treatment entails and what you can expect, including possible side effects.

We encourage you to ask questions at any time. Bringing a family member or friend along to your appointments can help you remember treatment details or key points you wanted to address.

Frequently asked questions about chemotherapy

  • How will my care team decide which chemotherapy drugs I need?

    Your care team will look at your cancer type, stage, overall health and treatment goals before recommending one or more chemotherapy drugs.

  • Can chemotherapy be combined with other treatments?

    Yes. Your doctor may recommend chemotherapy with surgery, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy or other treatments depending on your care plan.

  • Can someone come with me to chemotherapy appointments?

    The infusion center has space for a family member or friend to come with you for support. Your care team can share any current visitor guidance before your appointment.

Types of cancer we treat

We have decades of experience treating all types and stages of cancer, including rare and complex tumors. Our specialists work together to determine which chemotherapy drugs or combination of treatments are best for your situation.
